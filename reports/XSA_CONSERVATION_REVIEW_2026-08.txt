1. FIRE — one enormous December–February pulse, and it is people, not lightning
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3,990,865 detections in 38,725 fire groups, 2024–2026 (AOI window starts
2024-01-01). Only 300 groups (~0%) look like management burns. Peak: January.
Groups average 22 burn-days moving 6.7 km/day — fronts, not bonfires.

Nov–Feb carries ~87% of the annual load; Jul–Sep is effectively zero. The
front tracks the retreating rains N→S: the northern qoz belt burns first
(Oct–Nov, again Apr), the park belt at 5–8°N burns Dec–Feb. Direction of
spread is uniform (N/S/E/W all ~4,600–5,100 groups) — a landscape-scale
burning culture (hunting drives + pasture renewal), not point ignitions, and
no single migration axis to police.

  Like-for-like trend (NOAA-20 only, inside park boundary — avoids the 2024
  3-sensor fleet step; 2026 covers a full Jan–Mar burn season):

Story: SSD_Southern's burn load stepped up ~40% in 2024–25 on a same-sensor
basis — real, not the satellite fleet. Chinko is the only park whose interior
burning fell (2023→2025 −68%), consistent with active management, but 2026
rebounded to 2023 levels.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Cumulative 1,167 km² lost over 7,815 events (2001–2026) in the AOI — 0.24% of
the AOI in 25 years; by regional standards a LOW-deforestation landscape.

(a) The 2023 spike is QUESTIONED DATA, and the app says so: 48 events carrying
    281.7 of the 313.6 km² are flagged needs_review. They are all "logging"-
    classified linear blocks in four cells north of 10°N (10.1–10.8°N,
    23–25.2°E, Western Bahr el Ghazal fringe) — a 6× park-wide anomaly that is
    1,000× local. THE HISTORIC SHEETS NOW WEIGH IN (§6): that exact block is
    mapped a century ago as qoz dune country crossed by hunters' and caravan
    routes — `Qoz Dango`, `DARB EL FIL` ("the elephant road"), `Uninhabited
    forest`, `(Rough track)` — pastoral/forest ground with no settlement then
    and (§3) barely any now. Nothing in the historic or settlement record
    supports a 280 km² logging frontier there. Treat as "flagged, unverified,
    probably a map artefact", not as a logging finding.

    conservation victory. Ignore it; the sparkline cuts the line for a reason.

(c) The real, current story is the slash-and-burn belt along the Mbomou:
    the three largest 2026 events (4.4, 4.4, 3.8 km², hundreds of patches each,
    fire_ratio 220–350×) sit at 4.5–5.0°N between Zémio and Doruma — active
    agricultural conversion pressing on the CAR/DRC border forests, 8–60 km
    from named towns.
    https://five-megapixel-conservation.exe.xyz:8000/?aoi=XSA_Study_Area&lat=4.8&lng=27.0&z=8&from=2026-01-01

  Parks: Bili-Uere carries by far the most loss (602 km² cumulative, worst
  year 2022) — losing canopy ~3× faster than the unprotected landscape, the
  only park where that is true, and it is the wettest, rainforest-classed one:
  conversion there does not recover. Chinko is near-pristine (6.5 km² total,
  "improving"); Garamba trend "worsening" (worst year 2021, 3.2 km²).

3. SETTLEMENTS — the overgrazing proxy, now with measured persistence
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2,121 settlement clusters, 110.2 km² measured built surface, 2,633,521 people
(GHSL-measured for all 2,121 — no density constants).

  Classification:                      Persistence (GHSL E2000/E2015 epochs):
  temporary_camp  1,374  65%           permanent (pre-2000)   1,665  79%
  town              333  16%           established (2000-15)    208  10%
  village           311  15%           recent (post-2015)       248  12%
  settlement        102   5%
  agricultural        1   0%

(a) THE "CAMPS" ARE NOT TEMPORARY. v1 carried the caveat that persistence was
    NULL and camp-ness was a size classification. It is now measured:
    1,332 of the 1,374 "temporary_camp" clusters (97%) already had built
    surface in 2000; only 21 appeared after 2015. This is a PERSISTENT LATTICE
    of small settlements — and the histmaps name what it is (§6): the Dinka
    cattle-camp system (wun/wot/luak) the 1930s surveyors mapped in the same
    places. The fire association stands and now reads differently:
    median fires-within-5km — camps 1,285 vs towns 669; by persistence,
    permanent 1,264 vs recent 909. The OLDEST settlements sit deepest in the
    burning system: the fire is the long-standing pastoral economy of the old
    lattice, not something new arriving with new camps.

(b) WHERE THE CHANGE ACTUALLY IS: a post-2015 town belt at 8.5–9.4°N /
    26.8–28.2°E (Koko / Busseri River corridor, Western Bahr el Ghazal).
    150 of the 333 towns are post-2015; 62 of them sit in this one belt
    (~168,000 people). All recent clusters together hold ~569,000 people —
    22% OF THE AOI'S POPULATION LIVES IN SETTLEMENTS THAT DID NOT EXIST IN
    2015. This is the "north boundary pressure" of v1, with coordinates.

(c) AND IT IS RE-OCCUPATION, NOT COLONISATION. Cross-matched against the
    completed histmap gazetteer: 54% of recent clusters sit within 3 km of a
    named 1930s place vs 24% for random covered ground (2.2× random; higher
    than the 31% for permanent clusters). Of the 62 new WBeG towns, 36 sit on
    a named historic village (Rian Kuj, Karyat, Agok, Mayong, Fan Dung…).
    People are RETURNING to mapped villages — which changes the land-claims
    conversation entirely.

  Camps are everywhere, not just at the northern edge (count per 1° lat band):
  4°N 140 | 5°N 268 | 6°N 178 | 7°N 303 | 8°N 139 | 9°N 68 | 10°N 278

Settlement density inside parks stays 2–3× below the landscape. Chinko is the
counter-example in full: 20 settlements, 2.7 ha built surface, 826 people in
its ~20,000 km² — but pressure concentrates at its SSW boundary (Yalinga side).
Garamba's AOI slice has none.

4. ARTISANAL MINING — where the pits are, what they do NOT correlate with,
   and how long they have been there
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We hold no detector (retired 2026-08: optical 10 m ASM detection measured at
chance; turbidity/pit evidence withdrawn). What we hold is REPORTED sites:
3,494 reference sites across 4 countries; inside this AOI's bbox: 96
(40 Crisis Tracker incident-derived + 56 OSM; the OSM points cluster around
Juba-side quarries in the far east, the Crisis Tracker points in the west).

  Distance of reported mines to Chinko boundary:
  INSIDE          1   Kpangou area (6.18°N 23.34°E, gold+diamond nearby)
  < 5 km          1   Animogoli (3 km)
  5–25 km         5   Fode, Drombeko, Kpangou, Ammoungombi, Ngbanda-fringe
  25–60 km       12   incl. the OSM gold cluster W of the park (22.8°E)

  Two clusters matter:
  * SW corner of Chinko (5.4–6.4°N, 23.1–23.4°E): Kpangou — the one site with
    an extracted-commodity label in the AOI: "gold and diamond mine" — plus
    Fode, Ammoungombi (gold), Animogoli. This is the same corner the
    settlement narrative names for encroachment (Yalinga side).
  * Haute-Kotto / upper Kotto river (8.0–8.5°N, 23.3–23.9°E, N of the park):
    Yangou Dacko, Yangou Issi ×2, Yangou-Pendere ×2, Kotto 0/1/2, Voudou,
    Chantier Confluent — a dense field of repeatedly-attacked workings.
    Every one of these sites is in the list BECAUSE an armed group attacked
    it (Crisis Tracker selection rule): this cluster is armed-mining ground
    by construction.

  A CENTURY OF EXTRACTION AT THE SAME PITS (histmap cross-check, §6):
  * `Hofrat elNahas` ("the copper pit", 9.76°N 24.32°E) is printed on the
    colonial sheets; 8 km away sits Crisis Tracker site ct_015 — an unnamed
    mine where an LRA attack was reported in 2016. Same pit, worked and
    fought over, a century apart.
  * `Old copper workings` 9.11°N 23.86°E — 66 km from the nearest modern
    report, on the same Kotto-basin trend.
  * `(Iron mines)` 8.66°N 25.80°E and `Old iron forge` 7.38°N 26.34°E — no
    modern report within 200 km of either: genuinely dead, or outside
    reporting reach; we cannot distinguish.
  Where the historic and modern lists CAN be compared they agree — the sheets
  are an independent, pre-conflict mineral-occurrence layer for exactly the
  ground where modern reporting is thinnest.

  Conflict context (ACLED ADM1 scalars, 1996→now; province-scale only):

  TWO MEASURED NULLS WORTH HAVING:
  * Fire: density within 10 km of the mine sites is 7.3 detections/km² vs 8.4

  * Nightlights: VIIRS VNP46A3 monthly radiance at 71 reference sites

5. GEOLOGY — what the affinity model may and may not tell you here
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The geology layer is inference over lithology, scored against 7 truth sets.
For THIS area the honest summary is:

  CAR gold, units ("rocks that can host gold"):  lift 0.63×  — WORSE than
    random ground. Isolating gold-host units on CAR shows you LESS-worked
    ground. The pits sit on gneiss/syncinematic granite the table grades zero.
  CAR gold, junctions (contacts):  CONTESTED (violet in the UI):
    IPIS field visits say 2.18× (3.89× at classic floor); Tearline imagery
    census says 0.00× on the same claim. The panel says "the surveys
    disagree" and quotes the LOWEST number if forced to pick.
  Armed-presence stratum: gold-unit lift is 2.04× where IPIS recorded an armed
    actor at the pit vs 1.31× where not (p=0.0021 within-prefecture) — the
    model works BETTER exactly on armed-mining ground.
  Eastern CAR (= this AOI): UNMEASURED-to-null. The 41 Crisis-Tracker sites
    are the first ground truth east of 21°E (median 617 km from the nearest
    IPIS site); against them the sheet scores 0.89× (p=0.38, detectable range
    ≥1.23/≤0.73). On the east's rocks the model is not distinguishable from
    random — do not target patrols off amber dots here.

  Practical use that IS defensible in this AOI: junctions as a *question*
  (where to look next with imagery), never as a ranking; and the contacts +
  mining_anchors layers in the GeoPackage export for overlay with the Kotto
  and Kpangou clusters.

  https://five-megapixel-conservation.exe.xyz:8000/?geomap=car&geo_panel=1

6. THE HISTORIC BASELINE — the 1:250k sheets are complete, and they talk
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OCR of the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan Survey 1:250,000 series (editions 1908–1976,
median 1933) is COMPLETE: 187 sheets, 5,093 tiles, 98,055 label readings →
94,300 after dedup, 17,553 inside the AOI bbox (9,476 named places, 3,355
water features, 1,890 terrain, 735 vegetation, 468 route annotations) across
46 sheets. These maps are a century old — a HISTORIC REFERENCE, a snapshot of
the landscape before the modern conflict era, not a current gazetteer.
Georeferencing is good to a few hundred metres; the OCR is machine
transcription — verify against the sheet image before citing a name.

  COVERAGE CAVEAT: the series is Sudan. CAR/DRC park cores get border-sheet
  scraps only — Chinko 57 labels, Bili-Uere 26, Garamba 190, vs Southern NP
  751. Findings below apply to the Sudanese two-thirds; that the surveyors
  mapped the Chinko headwaters' rivers and jebels in detail and recorded
  almost no settlement agrees with the modern near-zero.

(a) THE PASTORAL GEOGRAPHY OF THE 1930s IS THE FIRE GEOGRAPHY OF THE 2020s.
    The sheets carry the surveyors' Dinka glossary in situ — `Toich = swamp
    formed by river flood`, `Wat = cattle enclosure`, `Wot: site of cattle
    camp, usually raised, with water adjacent`, `Luaks: large grass Dinka
    cattle byres`, `Dugdug: Dinka cattle camp` — and 40 place names beginning
    `Wun …` (Dinka wun, cattle-camp), 20 of them in the single degree square
    8–9°N / 28–29°E. The named `Open Toich` grazing floodplains at 7.9–8.5°N
    map exactly the belt where the fire front peaks in Nov–Dec (§1). The
    "temporary camp" lattice of §3 is this system, still standing where the
    surveyors drew it.

(b) THE VANISHED DENSITY OF THE BAHR EL GHAZAL. At 8.5°N 28°E (Gogrial/Jur)
    one 1:250k crop carries ~400 named settlements — against 23 OSM places in
    the whole 8°N band today. The post-2015 town belt of §3(b) sits exactly
    here, and §3(c) shows the returns land on the mapped villages. The
    century-old gazetteer is the best name source for the ground where modern
    maps are blankest.

(c) THE NORTHERN GRAZING BELT, VERBATIM: `Qoz Salsilgo`, `Qoz Binat`,
    `Uninhabited forest`, named rahads with dated water (`Sahabaia — water
    till end of Jan`, `Bahr — water plentiful Jan 1923`), tribal territories
    printed across the land (TAAISHA, FALLATA, BENIYA), `Umm Dafug (Watering
    place)`, `Buram (Police Post)`, `Telahun (Market)` — and the surveyor's
    own note on Qoz Dango: "Wells and Ruhud ... entered from hearsay. They
    mark hunters' routes across the Qoz north to south and east to west."
    A named, dated inventory of dry-season water points and grazing
    territories for exactly the belt where the transhumance originates.

(d) QUANTITATIVE CROSS-CHECKS against 2024–26 VIIRS fire (10 km boxes, random
    controls, 2,000-permutation test, 5–10°N / 24–31°E):

    historic feature                 n     det/km²  control  lift    p
    route annotations (darbs)      274      8.91     8.32   1.07×  0.03
    ironstone labels (flats etc)    54      9.89     8.25   1.20×  0.001

    The century-old route network still carries a real but weak (+7%) excess
    of burning; the ironstone laterite flats — the open grass surfaces of the
    Zande plateau — burn +20% over random ground. Neither is a targeting
    tool; both confirm the fire is structured by terrain and old geography,
    not by recent settlement.

7. WHAT I WOULD ACT ON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. GARAMBA'S 6% fire response rate is the weakest number in the corridor and
   it is a like-for-like measure. Worth a management question, not more data.
2. THE NORTH IS THE FRONT. SSD_Southern's +40% same-sensor burn step (2024–25)
   with 2,112 unstopped transits, plus the post-2015 WBeG town belt — 569,000
   people (22% of AOI population) in settlements that did not exist in 2015 —
   is the overgrazing/hunting front. But note §3(c): the growth is RETURN to
   villages mapped a century ago. Engagement framing: returning communities
   with historic claims, not encroachers.
3. CHINKO SW CORNER: reported gold+diamond workings inside/at the boundary,
   in park headwaters, on the same axis as its only settlement pressure.
   Small, specific, patrol-addressable — and invisible to every automated
   layer we run (no fire signal, no lights, no detector). Field/imagery
   follow-up is the only instrument.
4. ADJUDICATE the flagged 2023 "logging" block (281.7 km², north of 10°N)
   before anyone quotes the 2023 spike — the historic sheets say that ground
   was uninhabited qoz and caravan routes, which supports artefact over
   logging frontier.
5. HAUTE-KOTTO Yangou/Kotto cluster is the armed-mining watch zone — anchored
   on reported sites (attack-selected by construction), NOT on the geology
   drape, whose skill on eastern-CAR rocks is unmeasured-to-null (§5). Where
   dialogue reaches a pit, remember some of these workings are a century old
   (Hofrat el Nahas): extraction here predates every armed group present.
6. USE JANUARY. Every park's arrival peak, 87% of fire in Nov–Feb: the
   engagement calendar is Dec 15 – Feb 15, and the historic sheets name the
   dry-season water points that anchor it.

CAVEATS RIDING WITH EVERY NUMBER ABOVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Fire counts are detections inside boundaries (in_protected_area, re-derived
  2026-08-13); narrative totals count group-membership and are a different
  unit — both name their basis in the app.
* 2024+ raw fire series has a 3× instrument step (1→3 sensors); every trend
  quoted here is single-sensor like-for-like.
* Deforestation 2024+ is a different method (GFW); the series is cut, not
  comparable.
* Settlement persistence: "recent" = no GHSL built surface at 100 m in E2015;
  sub-resolution 2015 camps would also read "recent" (upper bound).
* Mine sites are REPORTED (incident/field lists with known reach bias), not
  detected; Crisis Tracker sites are armed-attack-selected by construction.
* Histmap labels are machine OCR of century-old sheets (Library of Congress
  g8310m.gct00289) — verify against the sheet image before citing a specific
  name; georeferencing good to a few hundred metres; Sudan coverage only.
* Route/ironstone fire lifts: 10 km sampling boxes, VIIRS 2024–26, 400 random
  controls, 2,000-permutation test; effects are weak-to-moderate and are
  context, not targeting.
* ACLED figures are our ADM1 sums of ACLED (Armed Conflict Location & Event
  Data) weekly aggregates, accessed 2026-08; analysis is ours, not ACLED's.
